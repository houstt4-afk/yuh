How each kind works
  • Misspelled — one or two small changes per word: a doubled letter, two letters swapped, a dropped vowel, a neighbouring key hit by mistake, or a sound spelled another way (ph for f, k for c). Still readable, just wrong.
  • Leetspeak — letters replaced by lookalike digits. Light touches o and l only; heavy swaps everything it can and mixes the case.
  • Wrapped — the words left alone, framed in symbols and separators.
  • Emoji — an emoji after each word it knows, or in place of it, or sparkles threaded between the words.
  • Other languages — common words swapped into Spanish, French, Italian, German or Japanese from a built-in list, so it's a mix rather than a real translation. When a word isn't in the list you get the -ish version instead: the English bent into that language's shape, which is a joke accent and not real words.
  • Numbers — digits standing in for letters, but only in one place at a time: first letters, last letters, vowels, middles, every other one, or the lot.
  • Reversed & clipped — backwards, each word backwards, word order flipped, half a word, vowels dropped, or every other letter.
  • Initials — the first letters, plain, dotted, spaced or slashed; the first two letters run together; and numeronyms, where the middle of a word becomes the count of letters it stands for, the way internationalization is written i18n.

Everything is generated on your device, and Generate gives you a fresh set each time.
